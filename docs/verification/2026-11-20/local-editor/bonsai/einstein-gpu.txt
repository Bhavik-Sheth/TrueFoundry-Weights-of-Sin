In a quiet corner of the Swiss Alps, nestled between snow-capped peaks and the whispering pines, there stood a small, unassuming house. It had no grand windows, no marble columns, and no sign that it was a place of genius. It was simply *Einstein’s*, as the locals called it—though no one ever quite knew who Einstein was, or where he came from.

The house was not built for fame. It was built for peace. A single wooden door, a stone fireplace, and a garden where wildflowers grew in the shade of ancient oaks. Inside, the air smelled of old books, tea, and the faintest trace of lavender—Einstein’s favorite scent, he once said, “It reminds me of the quiet before a storm.”

Einstein had not been born in a laboratory or under a starlit sky. He was born in 1879 in Ulm, a town where the river ran silver and the people spoke in hushed tones about the stars. His father was a schoolteacher, his mother a seamstress. They had no grand plans, only a love for books and the quiet hum of the world. But Einstein was different.
